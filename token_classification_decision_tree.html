Classification under SEC/CFTC Release Nos. 33-11412 & 34-105020 (Mar. 17, 2026)  ·  Revised

Step through six sequential questions to identify where a crypto asset falls within the SEC/CFTC five-category taxonomy. Questions are ordered by analytic priority per the release. Not legal advice — classification is fact-specific and requires counsel.

Investment contract overlay — applies to all non-security categories
Any non-security crypto asset can be offered and sold subject to an investment contract — which is a security — if the issuer induces an investment of money with representations or promises of essential managerial efforts from which purchasers would reasonably expect to profit. The token is not the security; the contract, transaction, or scheme is. The fact that a non-security token is subject to an investment contract does not transform the token itself into a security. §IV.A, p.24–27.

Secondary market transactions in a token remain securities transactions only for so long as purchasers would reasonably expect the issuer's representations or promises to remain connected to the token. This is an objective, facts-and-circumstances inquiry — not an automatic status that attaches to all secondary sales. The token separates from the investment contract upon: (1) fulfillment of the issuer's representations or promises; (2) public abandonment; or (3) a sufficiently long period of inaction that investors would no longer reasonably expect performance. §IV.B, p.28–31.

The offer and sale of an investment contract must be registered under the Securities Act or conducted pursuant to an available exemption, regardless of whether the underlying token later separates. Antifraud liability survives separation.
