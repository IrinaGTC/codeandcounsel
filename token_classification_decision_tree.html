Token Classification Decision Tree

Classification under SEC/CFTC Release Nos. 33-11412 & 34-105020 (Mar. 17, 2026)  ·  Read the classification guide below for full outcome descriptions.
Classification guide
Category V
Digital Security
Is a security
A financial instrument enumerated in the definition of "security" — stock, bond, note, equity interest — formatted as or represented by a crypto asset. A security is a security regardless of format or label. Debt-like tokens: additionally consider Reves v. Ernst & Young, 494 U.S. 56 (1990). Full Securities Act and Exchange Act obligations apply.
§III.E p.23–24
Category IV
Stablecoin
Three-tier analysis required
Tier 1 (Statutory): GENIUS Act permitted payment stablecoin — not a security by statute, effective upon implementing regulations. Tier 2 (Commission interpretation): Covered Stablecoin (fiat-backed, 1:1, no yield, fully reserved) — not a security, operative now. Tier 3: All others (crypto-collateralized, algorithmic, yield-bearing, DAO-governed) — may be a security depending on facts and circumstances.
§III.D p.21–23 · GENIUS Act §§2(22), 4(a)(11), 17
Caution
Further Analysis Required
Digital commodity status compromised
A token with exercisable utility on a functional system that also generates passive yield or conveys rights to future income, profits, or assets of a business enterprise does not fit the digital commodity definition. The disqualifying language is exact: digital commodities "do not have intrinsic economic properties or rights, such as generating a passive yield or conveying rights to future income, profits, or assets." Further analysis under the investment contract and digital security frameworks required. Consult counsel.
§III.A p.14
Category I
Digital Commodity
Not a security
A crypto asset native to a functional crypto system that derives its value from the programmatic operation of that system and supply and demand — not from essential managerial efforts of others. Must also lack passive yield and rights to future income. Examples named: BTC, ETH, SOL, ADA, XRP, AVAX, LINK, DOT, ALGO, DOGE, SHIB, HBAR, LTC, BCH, XLM, XTZ, APT. Strengthened by decentralization (no central party with operational/economic/voting control).
§III.A p.14–16 · n.49–50, n.54
Caution
May Be a Security
Investment contract analysis required
A fractionalized digital collectible, or one that enables fractional ownership of a single collectible, can constitute an investment contract. The Howey citrus grove analogy is direct: subdividing a single asset with centralized management converts what would otherwise be a simple sale into an investment contract. The underlying collectible remains a non-security; it is the fractionalized interest structure that requires securities analysis. Do not proceed without registered securities counsel.
§III.B p.19–20 · fn.66–67
Category II
Digital Collectible
Not a security
A crypto asset designed to be collected/used for artistic, entertainment, social, or cultural purposes — artwork, music, videos, trading cards, memes, in-game items. Value is based on subject matter, popularity, or scarcity. Creator royalties on resale do not change the analysis. Meme coins are a sub-type; they can later become digital commodities if they develop functional utility. Fan Tokens have hybrid characteristics (could also be digital tools).
§III.B p.16–20 · fn.57 (Fan Tokens)
Category III
Digital Tool
Not a security
A crypto asset performing a practical function — membership, ticket, credential, title instrument, identity badge — where value derives from functional utility, not expected profits from the developer's efforts. Often non-transferrable or soul-bound. Examples named: Ethereum Name Service domain names, NFT event tickets. Fan Tokens have hybrid characteristics and could fall here depending on facts.
§III.C p.20–21
Unclassified
Hybrid / Unclear
Counsel required — no default safe harbor
The release expressly acknowledges that some crypto assets may not fit any of the five categories and that others may have hybrid characteristics spanning multiple categories. There is no residual "not a security" safe harbor for unclassified tokens. The Howey test and the full statutory definition of "security" continue to apply. Fan Tokens are explicitly identified as one example of hybrid-category assets.
p.13
Investment contract overlay — applies to all non-security categories
Any non-security crypto asset can be offered and sold subject to an investment contract — which is a security — if the issuer induces an investment of money with representations or promises of essential managerial efforts from which purchasers would reasonably expect to profit. The token is not the security; the contract, transaction, or scheme is. §IV.A, p.24–27.

Secondary market transactions in a token remain securities transactions only for so long as purchasers would reasonably expect the issuer's representations or promises to remain connected to the token. This is an objective inquiry — not automatic for all secondary sales. The token separates from the investment contract upon: fulfillment of the issuer's promises; public abandonment; or a sufficiently long period of inaction. §IV.B, p.28–31.
